How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Emergency calls combine electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Blocking the discharge pipe, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are mistakes we see far too often.

Why call a technician instead of improvising? Certainty and safe sequencing. We verify the shutoffs, test for gas leaks with real instruments, and confirm the area is safe for electrical work. Then we find the root cause. A discharging relief valve often means high street pressure or no room for thermal expansion; a puddle might be a drain valve, a dielectric fitting, condensation, or a failed tank; a cold house may trace back to a thermocouple, an element, or a tripped high-limit switch. Each one has a different fix. We repair what can honestly be repaired, and when the tank is the problem, we explain your replacement options clearly and without a hard sell.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
