Not every water heater job is a straight replacement. Some Chicago, IL homeowners are finishing a basement or building an addition and need a second heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. Each of those jobs involves new gas piping, new vent routes, new circuits, or new water lines, and each one has to be planned before the first pipe is cut. We take on installations of every size, from a small point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Before any pipe is cut, we match the heater to your household. For tanks, first-hour rating counts more than gallons; for tankless, it is gallons per minute at your winter temperature rise. Then we pick the location with you. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. Heat pump models require breathing room, a space that stays above roughly 40 degrees, and a place to send condensate. Electric tankless models often require a dedicated high-amperage circuit. Anywhere a leak could ruin finished space, a drain pan goes underneath. You get clear choices and a firm price before work begins.

Installation day runs on a checklist: gas piping sized, run, fitted with a sediment trap, and leak-tested, venting sized and pitched to the manufacturer's spec, circuits and breakers matched to the heater's load, and water lines connected with shutoffs and dielectric protection between copper and steel. A full-size relief valve discharge line runs to a safe point, and any required expansion tank, drain pan, seismic straps, or permit is part of the job. Before we leave, we fill and purge the tank, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, set the temperature near 120 degrees, and walk you through everything. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

A little rust-colored water or a relief valve that drips now and then can be the first hint of a tank that is close to the end. If you notice any of these signs, contact Chicago Water Heater at (872) 206-1130 for a prompt inspection.

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Installation

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. We size by first-hour rating or flow rate, run and test the gas piping, route the vent to spec, match the circuit to the load, and add expansion tanks, drain pans, or straps where required. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. You end up with an installation that is safe, properly sized, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Glance at the pan and discharge pipe every month or two; water there means it is time to call
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • A periodic checkup with an anode inspection keeps a new tank healthy for years
